\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data and protocol.} We use the public Criteo conversion logs released
with~\cite{chapelle2014modeling}, the standard corpus for this problem. Clicks span 60 days.
Days $[0,30)$ initialise the model causally; days $[30,60)$ are the streaming period, divided
into \nCohorts{} hourly rounds. At each round the model trains on the records released in the
preceding hour and then scores every click of the following hour exactly once, giving
\nEval{} scored clicks at a base conversion rate of \baseRate{}. Learning rates are selected per
arm and per cell from a \nLr{}-point grid on a held-out slice of the initialisation period,
before any streaming evaluation, and pinned across seeds.

\paragraph{Why one attribution window.} We report $v=1$\,day only. A short click-attribution
window is the common setting for click-through conversion optimisation, and a single window
keeps every comparison origin-matched --- arms are compared only against arms trained and
scored on the same clicks against the same target. The cost is that our conclusions hold for
short windows (Sect.~\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\paragraph{Axes.} \emph{Served capacity} takes five values from \capMin{} to
\capMax{} parameters, obtained by scaling the embedding tables and the interaction trunk
together; the ladder spans more than three orders of magnitude so that a result holding at one model
size alone cannot masquerade as general. \emph{Write delay} $o$ takes $5$, $30$ and $90$ minutes,
covering $F(o)$ from \foFive{} to \foNinety{}. Every arm is run at every combination with five seeds. The full one-day reproduction is
\nArms{} arms, \nCells{} cells and \nRuns{} runs; this paper analyses the four axis arms of
Sect.~\ref{sec:axis}, the two references, and the frozen-teacher variants of
Sect.~\ref{sec:results}.

\paragraph{Metric.} We report normalised entropy,
\begin{equation}
  \NE \;=\; \frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum_i \big[\,y_i\log \hat p_i + (1-y_i)\log(1-\hat p_i)\,\big]}
                 {\bar y\log\bar y + (1-\bar y)\log(1-\bar y)},
\end{equation}
with $y_i=\Yv_i$, $\bar y$ the base rate, lower better. $\NE$ is pooled over all \nEval{}
scored clicks rather than averaged over rounds, since a per-round average normalises each hour
by its own base rate and discards diurnal variation a model can legitimately predict. Our
headline quantity is the difference $\dNE$ between two arms in one cell: absolute $\NE$ depends
on corpus and capacity, whereas the question is which target wins at fixed everything-else.

\paragraph{Statistics.} The five seeds differ only in initialisation and minibatch order --- the
data is identical across them --- so seed $i$ of two arms is a matched pair and we use a paired
$t$-test within each cell, correcting across the \nContrast{} cells of a contrast with the
Holm--Bonferroni step-down procedure. We never pool across cells: different $o$ means a
different label distribution. Seed dispersion is small at every rung (median $\NE$ standard
deviation \seedSdMed{}, maximum \seedSdMax{} over the arms studied), so cells are individually
informative. Five seeds measure initialisation and shuffle variance on one data origin ---
precision, not generalisation --- and we claim nothing beyond this corpus and split.
