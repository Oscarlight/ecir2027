\section{Setting and the Single-Visit Constraint}
\label{sec:setting}

\subsection{Streaming protocol}

A click $i$ occurs at time $C_i$ with features $x_i$ and, if it converts, does so after a delay
$D_i>0$ ($D_i=\infty$ otherwise). An \emph{attribution window} $v$ fixes the business target,
$\Yv_i=\mathbf{1}[D_i\le v]$, knowable only at $C_i+v$. A streaming pipeline cannot wait that
long, so it writes the record at a \emph{write delay} $o\ll v$ carrying the outcome visible
then:
\begin{equation}
  \Yo_i \;=\; \mathbf{1}\!\left[D_i \le o\right] \;\le\; \Yv_i .
  \label{eq:labels}
\end{equation}
$\Yo$ is never a false positive and often a false negative; how often is
$F(o)=P(D\le o\mid D\le v)$, the share of the target's positives visible at write time. At
$v=1$\,d on our corpus $F(o)=\foFive$, $\foThirty$, $\foNinety$ for $o=5$, $30$, $90$ minutes:
at a five-minute pipeline two thirds of the conversions that will count are still missing.
Training runs in hourly rounds --- consume the records released since the last cut-off, then
score every click of the next hour once, retrospectively against $\Yv$ --- so every method is
judged on the true business label whatever it trained on, and update-then-predict keeps the
evaluation causal.

\subsection{Two admissibility rules}

\textbf{Rule 1 (causality).} A record's label must be computable when the record is written
and must not change afterwards. Labelling a record $\Yv$ while writing it at $C_i+o$ uses an
outcome that has not happened --- an upper reference (\textsc{Oracle}), not a system. Strict.

\textbf{Rule 2 (single-visit).} Writing $n_i$ for the number of times click $i$ enters the
served model's loss over its lifetime, Rule~2 requires $\max_i n_i\le 1$: a strong practical
constraint, being the property that decides whether a method drops into an append-only pipeline
unchanged.

The criterion is $\max_i n_i$, not the mean: it is the \emph{existence} of a second write, not
its frequency, that obliges the pipeline to support one. And the rule governs the \emph{served} model's stream: auxiliary estimators (importance
weights, delay heads, teachers) are not charged, because the served stream is what the pipeline
must materialise. Under a stricter reading, in which any auxiliary fitted on matured labels
counts as a second visit, the screen would eliminate \textsc{Fsiw}, \textsc{Ulc} \emph{and
both of our own soft-target arms} alike.

\subsection{The screen}

protocol, the training records per click their served model consumes at $v=1$\,d.\footnote{Two measurement traps, both of which understate duplication.
Dividing records consumed by records released conflates duplication with traffic variation,
since a maturity-gated stream reads clicks from $v$ ago when the click rate differed; the
denominator must be \emph{distinct clicks touched}. And a window shorter than a method's revisit
horizon cannot see the revisit, so we score only clicks whose entire horizon lies inside the
stream. Duplication is a property of emission logic, not of the model: re-running the audit at
the smallest and largest capacities of Sect.~\ref{sec:setup} gives identical counts.}

\nFail{} of \nScreened{} methods write more than one record for at least one click, and the
factors are not marginal: \textsc{Ftp} consumes \maxDup{} records per click, \textsc{Miss}
\missDup{}, and \maxVisitsArm{} reaches \maxVisits{} writes for a single click, its mechanism
being an update applied whenever a label reverses. Only \passList{} are single-visit. The plain
re-ingestion baseline --- write the click as a negative, write a positive when the conversion
lands~\cite{ktena2019addressing,gu2021real} --- is not a named correction and so is not listed,
but our implementation measures \vanillaDup{} records per click and fails the same criterion.

Of the survivors, \textsc{Fsiw} corrects the \emph{loss} and \textsc{Twice} adds a head on
the conversion clock; both leave the record's target untouched. \textsc{Ulc} alone changes the
target, making it the comparator for the axis we study next --- not because the other two are
inferior, but because varying three axes at once would attribute nothing.
