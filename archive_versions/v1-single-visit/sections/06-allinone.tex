\section{Toward Cross-Funnel Consolidation}
\label{sec:allinone}

The results above concern one objective; our motivation is several. This section states the
connection and its limits; it is an argument, not a measurement.

\paragraph{Why heterogeneous windows block consolidation.}
Consolidating many ranking objectives into one model is an active industrial
direction~\cite{meta2025gem}. Consider one model serving $K$ conversion objectives --- a
purchase, an app install, a lead --- with attribution windows $v_1,\dots,v_K$ differing by
orders of magnitude. Under \textsc{Wait}, objective $k$'s record can only be written at $C_i+v_k$, when its label
exists, so the $K$ heads want records at $K$ different times. A shared pipeline cannot honour
that with one write per click: it must either emit a record per objective, which multiplies the
stream by $K$ and violates Rule~2 $K$ times over, or delay every objective to
$\max_k v_k$, which imposes the slowest funnel's staleness on the fastest.

What blocks consolidation is therefore the hard matured label, not the architecture.

\paragraph{What a write-time target changes.}
Both targets we advocate are computable at a single common write time. At $C_i+o$ the pipeline
can evaluate, for every objective $k$ simultaneously,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde y_{i,k} \;=\; \Yo_{i,k} + \left(1-\Yo_{i,k}\right) \wt_k(x_i),
\end{equation}
because $\wt_k$ is a function the pipeline can call rather than an outcome it must await. One
record, written once at one time, can then carry all $K$ targets. The heterogeneity in $v_k$ is
absorbed into the $K$ teachers, each of which reconciles its own window offline, and the served
pipeline becomes window-agnostic: it sees features and numbers.

\paragraph{What distinguishes the teacher from the online corrector.}
\textsc{Ulc} also evaluates its corrector at write time, so it achieves the same alignment, and
our results show it does so at the same accuracy. The difference is where the per-objective
machinery lives. \textsc{Ulc} fits each $w_k$ as an auxiliary \emph{inside} the served model's
training loop, so consolidating $K$ objectives means $K$ auxiliaries coupled to one optimiser,
each with its own maturity-gated input and its own failure modes. The teacher route places each
$w_k$ in a separate model upstream, whose only interface to the served pipeline is a number
attached to a record. That is an argument about modularity, not about $\NE$.

How far that reaches is decided by the frozen-teacher measurement of
Sect.~\ref{sec:results}, and the answer is conditional. For write delays of 30 minutes or more
the teacher can be frozen at negligible cost, so each $w_k$ becomes a build-time artifact --- a
model trained once, shipped, and queried --- and consolidating $K$ objectives adds $K$
artifacts rather than $K$ live maturity-gated services. At a five-minute pipeline with a large
served model the teacher must stay live, and the teacher route's operational advantage over
\textsc{Ulc} narrows to decoupling alone.

\paragraph{What this evidence does not establish.}
We ran one objective, so we have no evidence about interference between objectives sharing a
trunk --- the central risk of consolidation, and what a multi-task study would have to measure.
We have no evidence that $K$ teachers are affordable, only that one is: teacher capacity is
training-only but not free, and \teacherParams{} parameters per objective is a real cost. And
our single objective has a short window, the easy case; at $v=28$~days the teacher's own labels
are far staler, and the argument would need re-testing rather than extrapolating.

What we claim is narrower and, we think, still worth reporting: under a constraint that
eliminates most published methods, a target computable at write time is available that costs
nothing against the status quo, and the most pipeline-convenient version of it --- the one that
carries no hard label at all --- is the one that does not work. That is a negative result
cheaper to learn on one objective than on $K$.
